# industrial-craft — Custom (trades / manufacturing)

**Vibe**: Tactile precision and mechanical reliability — the control panel of a well-made physical tool, not a screen.
**Best for**: trades, manufacturing, auto/equipment repair, contractors and hardware-adjacent businesses — tone words like "industrial", "technical", "mechanical", "rugged", "precise", "functional".

## Palette approach
- Neutral, material-evoking grounds — brushed-metal grays, matte off-white, charcoal — rather than bright brand-color fills.
- The real brand primary/accent colors are used for functional signals: status indicators, active states, a safety-style accent color used the way warning-orange is used on real equipment.
- Consistent top-left "lighting" direction implied through subtle gradients on raised elements, not flat color blocks.

## Typography
- Display: a condensed or technical-feeling sans, confident weight -- Oswald fits well.
- Body: Work Sans at standard weight, generous line-height for readability against denser layouts.
- Labels/specs in a monospace or semi-condensed face, evoking a spec sheet -- JetBrains Mono or Archivo Narrow.

## Spacing
Tighter and more structured than a warm/editorial recipe — a clear grid, moderate section padding rather than airy whitespace.

## Radius
Small and consistent (4-8px) — panels and cards read as machined parts, not soft app cards.

## Shadow
Directional and structural rather than decorative — a consistent light-source shadow that implies physical depth on raised panels/buttons, not a soft ambient blur.

## Motion
Mechanical and precise — short, deliberate transitions (150-250ms), a pressed/depressed feel on buttons (slight translate + shadow inversion) rather than a lift-and-glow.

## Signature moves
- Small structural details that read as "manufactured": subtle corner treatments, ventilation-slot-style dividers, a status-indicator dot pattern.
- Spec-sheet-style presentation of services/capabilities (label + value pairs) rather than soft marketing card copy.
- A confident, functional CTA style — solid, high-contrast, no soft gradient.

## Avoid
- Soft pastel palettes or rounded-pill buttons — reads as the wrong category entirely.
- Purely decorative gradients unrelated to a light-source metaphor.
- Hand-drawn or whimsical illustration.
